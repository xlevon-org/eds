A3

What is a spreadsheet model?

A spreadsheet model allows the user to change variables in a spreadsheet in order to recreate a real situation and predict the result of a situation or scenario.

Examples of simple models

These models use arithmetic formulas or spreadsheet functions:

  • The potential profit or loss that could be made by a new shop selling milkshakes.
  • The potential cost of decorating a home by pricing materials and equipment.
  • The potential grade outcomes of students based on previous assessments.

Real-world example

Families sometimes create a simple spreadsheet model to record their weekly spending. They can use this to plan ahead and look for ways to save money.

Variable: a value in the model that you can change, such as a price or a distance. When a variable changes, every formula that uses it updates by itself.

Formula or function?

A formula is a calculation you write yourself using cell references and operators, such as =F2-F1.

A function is a ready-made formula with a name. You give it a range of cells and it does the work, such as =SUM(B2:B13).

Reading a range: B2:B13 means “every cell from B2 down to B13”.

A simple financial model

This model calculates the cost of building a computer for a small computer shop and the potential profit.

Build total

Add up the price of every component.
=SUM(B1:B10)

Total cost

Build total plus the staff build cost.
=B11+D1

Profit

Retail price minus total cost.
=D3-D2

In the book’s example the profit is −$60. The cost of building the computer is higher than the retail price, so the shop would make a loss.

The goal seek function

Spreadsheet software includes a goal seek function that can adjust a variable until a desired outcome is created. You choose the answer you want, and the spreadsheet works backwards to find the value that gives it.

What if vs goal seek

What ifGoal seek
You change a value and look at the result.You choose the result and the spreadsheet finds the value.
“What if the staff cost is $100?”“What staff cost gives a profit of $50?”

Where to find it

  • Microsoft Excel: Data → What-If Analysis → Goal Seek
  • LibreOffice Calc: Tools → Goal Seek

You fill in three boxes: the cell with the answer (Set cell), the answer you want (To value), and the cell it may change (By changing cell).

Goal Seek
Goal Seeking Status

Goal seek challenges

Start each challenge from the book’s values (click “Back to the book’s values” in A7).

  1. Set D4 to 50 by changing D1. What staff build cost gives a $50 profit?
  2. Set D4 to 100 by changing D3. What retail price gives a $100 profit?
  3. Set D4 to 0 by changing D3. What is the lowest retail price with no loss?
  4. Set D4 to 100 by changing D1. Why is the answer impossible in real life?
    Show answerGoal seek gives a staff build cost of −$10. A cost cannot be less than $0, so the shop cannot make $100 profit by only changing the staff cost. It must also buy cheaper components or raise the retail price.
A9

Retail and wholesale prices

In the financial model, the profit from a computer is calculated by taking away the total build cost from the retail price. This is correct if we are buying products directly from the people that make them.

However, supermarkets don’t make all the food they sell, and a website selling laptops may not have built them all themselves. Companies that buy products from other companies, so they can sell them to us, buy their products at a wholesale price.

Retail price

Retailers sell products directly to us, the consumers, at a retail price.

It is more expensive than wholesale because it includes the price the retailer paid to buy the product and their costs to be able to sell it, such as a shop, staff or website.

Wholesale price

Wholesalers sell products only to retail companies at a wholesale price.

It is cheaper because retail companies buy lots of stock, for example fifty computers instead of one.

Sort it: retail or wholesale?
